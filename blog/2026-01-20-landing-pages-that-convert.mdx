A landing page has one job. A visitor arrives from an ad, an email or a search result with a specific need, and the page either convinces them to take the next step or loses them. There is no navigation to explore, no "about us" to fall back on. It is the most measurable page on your website, and often the one that matters most to your marketing budget.

Good landing pages are not a matter of design taste. They follow a small set of principles that marketers and developers both need to understand. Here they are, with a checklist at the end.

## Match the message

The headline should continue the conversation the visitor started. If the ad said "Hire a remote call center team in two weeks," the page should say that too, not "Welcome to our global talent platform." Visitors decide in seconds whether they are in the right place, and mismatched messages make them leave.

That often means one landing page per campaign or audience, rather than sending every ad to the homepage. With a reusable template, creating variations is quick.

## One page, one goal

Every landing page should have a single primary action: book a call, request a quote, start a trial, download a guide. Everything on the page should support that action.

- Remove the main site navigation, or reduce it to the logo
- Repeat the same call to action several times down the page, not different ones
- Cut links that lead elsewhere unless they help the decision, such as a pricing explanation

A page with five equally prominent options usually converts worse than a page with one.

## The structure that works

Most effective landing pages follow a similar order:

1. **Headline and subheadline** stating the outcome, matched to the traffic source
2. **Primary call to action** visible without scrolling
3. **Proof:** client logos, short testimonials, ratings or numbers you can support
4. **How it works** in three or four steps
5. **Benefits,** framed around the visitor's problem rather than your features
6. **Objections answered:** pricing, timelines, risk, commitment
7. **Final call to action**

Proof must be real. Invented testimonials or logos of companies you have not worked with damage trust far more than they help, and they can create legal problems.

## Forms: ask for less

Every extra field reduces the number of people who finish the form. Ask only for what you need to take the next step, and collect the rest later. A name, email and one qualifying question is often enough.

- Label every field clearly, above the field rather than inside it
- Show helpful error messages next to the field, not at the top of the page
- Say what happens after submitting: "We'll reply within one business day"
- Test the form on a phone with real typing, not just in a desktop browser

## Speed is part of conversion

Visitors from paid ads are impatient, and a slow page wastes money you have already spent on the click. Landing pages should be among the fastest pages you have:

- Compress and correctly size images, especially the hero image
- Avoid heavy sliders, autoplay videos and large animation libraries
- Load chat widgets and tracking scripts after the main content
- Check Core Web Vitals on a mobile connection, as described in our [technical SEO checklist](https://zemenaytech.com/blog/technical-seo-checklist-new-website)

## Design for phones first

For many campaigns, most visitors arrive on a phone. Check that the headline and call to action appear without scrolling on a small screen, that buttons are large enough to tap, and that nothing important hides behind a pop-up.

## Track it properly

A landing page is only as useful as its measurement. Before launch, confirm that:

- Campaign parameters from ads and emails are captured
- Form submissions and bookings are tracked as conversions in analytics and ad platforms
- Submissions arrive in the CRM with the campaign source attached
- Cookie consent works as your policy requires

The broader pre-launch checks are in our [website launch checklist](https://zemenaytech.com/blog/website-launch-checklist).

## Test one thing at a time

Once the page has steady traffic, run tests on the elements with the biggest effect: the headline, the call to action, the form length and the proof near the top. Change one thing at a time, and run each test long enough to reach a meaningful number of conversions rather than stopping after a good day.

## Build them so marketing can move fast

The best setup lets marketers create and edit landing pages from approved components without waiting for a developer, while developers keep control of performance and tracking. That is a platform decision as much as a design one; see [Next.js vs WordPress vs Webflow](https://zemenaytech.com/blog/nextjs-vs-wordpress-vs-webflow) for the trade-offs.

## The checklist

- Headline matches the ad, email or search query
- One primary call to action, repeated
- Navigation removed or minimized
- Real, specific proof near the top
- Short form with clear labels and a stated next step
- Fast on a mobile connection
- Readable and usable on a phone
- Conversions tracked end to end, with campaign source in the CRM
- A plan for the first test
