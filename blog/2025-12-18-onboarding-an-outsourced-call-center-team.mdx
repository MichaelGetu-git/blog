Most outsourced call center problems that show up in month three were created in week one. The agents were given a login, a product demo and a script, and sent onto the phones. They learned the rest from customers, one mistake at a time.

A structured first month avoids almost all of that. It does not need to be elaborate. It needs to be deliberate, with clear owners on your side and the provider's side, and a short list of things that must be true before each step.

## Before day one: the preparation week

The work that matters most happens before any agent starts.

**Name an owner on your side.** One person who answers agent questions, approves call flows and attends the weekly review. Without this person, questions go unanswered and agents start guessing.

**Gather the knowledge.** Pull together everything your best in-house agent knows: product documentation, pricing, policies, refund rules, escalation paths, common questions and their answers. It will be incomplete. That is fine; the first month exists to fill the gaps.

**Set up access.** Telephony, CRM or help desk, knowledge base, internal chat. Use individual accounts, never shared logins, and give the minimum permissions the role needs. Test every login before day one; access problems are the most common reason first days are wasted.

**Agree on success.** Write down the targets the team will be measured against at the end of the month, such as service level, first call resolution, CSAT and quality score, and the date they become binding. New teams should not be held to full targets in week one.

## Week one: learning the business

Agents spend this week learning, not taking calls.

- **Product and company:** what you sell, who buys it and why, and what customers usually call about
- **Systems:** hands-on practice in the CRM and phone system with test records
- **Policies:** refunds, exceptions, escalations and anything agents must never promise
- **Call flows:** the structure of each call type, with the exact compliance wording highlighted

End the week with a short test. Not a formality, but a real check of whether each agent can find answers in the knowledge base and explain your core policies in their own words. Anyone who struggles gets extra time before moving on.

## Week two: listening and role-play

Agents listen to recorded calls from your existing team, good ones and difficult ones, and discuss what happened. Then they practice: team leads play customers with common problems, then harder ones. Each agent should handle every major call type in role-play at least twice before taking it live.

This is also when you will discover what your documentation is missing. Every question an agent cannot answer from the knowledge base is a gap. Log them in one shared place and have your owner answer them within a day.

## Week three: supervised live calls

Agents go live with a safety net:

- **Start with the simplest call types**, or with a share of overflow volume rather than the whole queue
- **Team leads listen in** on a sample of calls and coach immediately afterwards
- **Escalation is easy and encouraged.** New agents should never feel they have to bluff
- **Daily huddles** of fifteen minutes cover what went wrong yesterday and what changed in the knowledge base

Expect handle times to be long and resolution rates to be lower than your in-house team's. That is normal. What you are watching for is the trend.

## Week four: full queue and first review

Agents take the full range of calls they will handle permanently. QA scoring starts in earnest, using the scorecard agreed in the preparation week.

At the end of the month, hold a proper review with both sides in the room:

- How each agreed metric compares with its target, and why
- The ten most common reasons customers called, and which ones a product or website change could prevent
- Knowledge gaps that still exist
- Which agents are ready for more complex call types, and which need more support
- What the provider needs from you that they are not getting

That review sets the rhythm for every month that follows. For the metrics themselves, see [call center KPIs that actually matter](https://zemenaytech.com/blog/call-center-kpis-that-matter).

## The mistakes that undo onboarding

- **Skipping the listening week** because volume is high. The calls you save in week two you pay back in week six.
- **Changing the call flow daily.** Collect changes and release them weekly so agents are not learning a moving target.
- **No owner on the client side.** Agents cannot fix what nobody will explain to them.
- **Judging the team on day-ten numbers.** Judge them on the trend across the month.
