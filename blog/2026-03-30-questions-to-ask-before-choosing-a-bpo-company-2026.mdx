# 10 Questions to Ask Before Choosing a BPO Company

Choosing a BPO company is not like buying ordinary software.

{/* truncate */}

Your provider may interact directly with your customers, access internal systems, process sensitive information and become responsible for a large part of the customer experience.

That means the right questions go beyond **"What is your hourly rate?"**

A 2026 BPO evaluation guide argues that standard procurement criteria—price, location, certifications, references, technology and SLAs—are necessary but not sufficient. Buyers also need to understand how a provider handles exceptions, operational variance and consistency over time. ## 1. Have you handled a program like ours before?

This is one of the most important questions.

Ask about:

- Industry
- Customer type
- Contact volume
- Channels
- Languages
- Complexity
- Team size
- Coverage hours

A provider may have ten years of BPO experience but no experience with your actual workflow.

### What to request

Ask for:

- Case studies
- Client references
- Sample KPIs
- Average tenure
- Program size
- Typical onboarding timeline

## 2. Who will actually manage our account?

The sales team may be excellent.

But who will run the operation?

Meet:

- Account manager
- Operations manager
- QA manager
- Training lead
- Workforce manager
- IT/security lead

Ask how frequently you will interact with them.

A strong governance structure is one of the biggest differences between a supplier and a true operating partner.

## 3. How do you recruit and retain agents?

Ask:

- Where do agents come from?
- How are they screened?
- What skills are tested?
- What is average tenure?
- What is annual attrition?
- How quickly can vacancies be filled?
- What happens during hiring shortages?

High turnover can destroy customer experience even when the provider's hourly rate looks attractive.

## 4. What does training look like?

Do not accept "we provide comprehensive training."

Ask for the actual program.

Find out:

- Training duration
- Product training
- System training
- Security training
- Communication training
- Certification tests
- Nesting period
- Ongoing coaching

Then ask:

**"What percentage of agents pass certification before handling live customers?"**

## 5. How do you measure quality?

Quality assurance should be systematic.

Ask to see a sample scorecard.

It might include:

- Accuracy
- Compliance
- Empathy
- Process adherence
- Resolution
- Communication
- Documentation

Also ask:

**"How many interactions does QA review per agent?"**

And:

**"How do you calibrate scoring with clients?"**

## 6. What happens when an agent makes a mistake?

This question reveals operational maturity.

Ask for examples of how the provider handles:

- Wrong information
- Policy violations
- Customer complaints
- Security incidents
- Escalation failures
- Repeated quality issues

Excellent providers should have a documented corrective-action process.

## 7. What security and compliance controls do you have?

Ask about:

- Access control
- MFA
- Encryption
- Device security
- Call recording
- Data retention
- Employee access
- Incident response
- Business continuity
- Relevant certifications

Your exact requirements will depend on your industry and customer data.

Do not outsource compliance responsibility simply because the provider says "we are compliant."

Your legal and security teams should review the actual controls and contract.

## 8. What exactly is included in your price?

This question prevents unpleasant surprises.

Ask whether the quote includes:

- Agents
- Supervisors
- QA
- Training
- Recruitment
- Technology
- Telephony
- Reporting
- Management
- Holiday coverage
- Overtime
- Setup
- Integration

Then ask for all possible additional fees.

### Compare total cost, not headline rate

For example:

| Cost item | Vendor A | Vendor B |
|---|---:|---:|
| Agent rate | $12 | $14 |
| Setup | $10k | $2k |
| QA | Included | $1/hr |
| Technology | Included | $2/hr |
| Management | Included | Included |
| Holiday coverage | +20% | Included |

Vendor B's higher headline rate may actually produce a lower total cost.

## 9. How quickly can you scale?

Ask for a specific scenario.

**"If our volume doubles in 60 days, how many agents can you add?"**

Then ask:

- Where will they come from?
- Who trains them?
- How long until certification?
- Will quality change?
- Can supervisors scale too?

Scaling agents without scaling management and QA is a common failure point.

## 10. What happens if we want to leave?

This is one of the most important questions and one of the most frequently ignored.

Ask about:

- Notice period
- Data return
- Data deletion
- Knowledge transfer
- Phone numbers
- Software accounts
- Customer records
- Transition assistance
- Early termination fees
- Staff transfer

A good contract should make the exit process understandable before you enter the relationship.

## Bonus question: How do you handle exceptions?

Average transactions are easy to demonstrate.

Real customer support is full of exceptions.

Ask:

**"Show us how your team handles a case that does not fit the standard process."**

This tests:

- Judgment
- Escalation
- Documentation
- Manager involvement
- Customer empathy

A provider that can explain its exception-handling process is usually more mature than one that only talks about average handle time.

## BPO selection scorecard

After interviews, score every vendor consistently.

| Category | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Relevant experience | 15% |
| Quality | 15% |
| Staffing/attrition | 10% |
| Security | 15% |
| Technology | 10% |
| Pricing/TCO | 15% |
| Scalability | 5% |
| Business continuity | 5% |
| References | 5% |

Do not let a vendor win because it is cheapest in one category.

## Red flags when choosing a BPO company

Watch for:

- Refusal to provide references
- Unusually low pricing without explanation
- Vague security answers
- High unexplained attrition
- No dedicated operational owner
- No measurable QA process
- Heavy sales involvement but little delivery detail
- Pressure to sign before due diligence
- No clear exit process
- "We can do anything" without evidence

A 2026 outsourcing partner due-diligence guide specifically warns that choosing on price alone can hide weaknesses in staffing, QA, management or delivery infrastructure. [Workanova](https://workanova.com/5-mistakes-companies-make-when-choosing-an-outsourcing-partner-for-customer-support/)

## What should you request before signing?

Ask for a due-diligence package containing:

- Company information
- Financial/business stability evidence
- Relevant references
- Security documentation
- Business-continuity plan
- Training curriculum
- QA methodology
- Staffing model
- Pricing sheet
- SLA draft
- Data-processing terms
- Exit terms

The objective is not paperwork for its own sake.

The objective is to reduce surprises after launch.

## FAQ

### What is the most important question to ask a BPO?

Ask whether they have successfully operated a program with the same type of work, volume, complexity and customer expectations as yours.

### Should price determine which BPO I choose?

No. Price matters, but total cost of ownership and operational performance matter more.

### How many BPO companies should I shortlist?

Three to five strong candidates is usually enough for a serious comparison. A smaller, qualified shortlist is better than evaluating dozens of weak matches.

### Should I ask for client references?

Yes. Ask for references from clients with similar scale and complexity, not only the provider's biggest or most famous customer.

### What is the biggest BPO selection mistake?

Choosing a provider before defining your own requirements. If your scope, volume, KPIs and security needs are unclear, vendor comparisons become unreliable.

## Final takeaway

The goal of **choosing a BPO company** is not to find the provider with the best sales presentation.

You want the provider that can repeatedly deliver the required customer outcome, at a predictable total cost, with acceptable security and enough operational maturity to handle problems when things go wrong.

Ask these ten questions, verify the answers with evidence, and test the shortlisted provider with a realistic pilot before committing significant customer volume.
