# The Resume-Driven Development Trap: Why Boring Tech Wins

Shiny-object syndrome has been a challenge for all engineering organisations. You just finished your new SaaS product and the blog post about how one of the big tech companies served 10 million requests per second using their entirely new distributed graph database lands on your desk!

That's Resume-Driven Development: picking cutting edge technology, simply because it's cool to include on a LinkedIn profile. Distributed Engineering Teams' silent killers are hype driven architecture. When introducing experimental frameworks in the context of scaling through Offshore Software Development, huge knowledge silos are the result. With one Senior Engineer that knows your arcane message broker, Remote Team Management is a hostage situation and your Remote Work Culture starts to fracture over hero-worship instead of collective ownership.

## There's a hidden tax on hype

Unproven technology has a high operational tax which slows down each layer of delivery:

- Tools that are bleeding edge are not well-documented and community doesn't have answers for them. In production, engineers jump the rules of the game, that is, they violate the principles of Asynchronous Work Strategies and are forced to go through a war room which may be multi-time zone and through which they have to reverse engineer the whole organization to be able to conduct a research on a possible fix when a bug occurs.
- With more experimental libraries in your stack, the longer Remote Developer Onboarding takes. New hires are not given any product code in week one, but are instead given to work with undocumented configuration files for a month.
- The low morale of developers is due to the infrastructure fragility and lack of support, while low numbers of Remote Talent Retention do not even break a sweat. Top engineers do not want to be involved in doing alpha open source work around the weekend to fix their problems with hard products.
- International Tech Compliance has big blind spots: When it comes to audit nightmares, unproven tools have a high risk of not being enterprise-grade and don't always come with logging and security certifications.

## The "Boring" Blueprint

At the highest performing Distributed Agile Teams, they are deliberately going for boring, battle-tested technology. PostgreSQL, standard REST or GraphQL APIs, Redis and well established runtimes in the backend have taken care of decades of production edge-cases.

For one, no need to learn the architecture language, and boring tech is a superpower for Cross-Cultural Team Integration. It has a lot of documentation, answers to problems occur on Stack Overflow and security patches are expected. If you agree and standardize and choose tools that have proven to deliver value to the customer, 90% of your engineer's effort is spent adding value to the customer and 10% to the plumbing.
