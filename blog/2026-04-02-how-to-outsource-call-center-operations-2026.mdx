# How to Start Outsourcing Your Call Center Operations

If your call volumes are growing, your internal managers are overwhelmed, or you need longer coverage hours, it may be time to **outsource call center operations**.

{/* truncate */}

But outsourcing a call center is not simply transferring phone calls to another company.

A professional outsourced operation involves staffing, workforce management, telephony, training, quality assurance, reporting, escalation management, business continuity and customer-experience governance.

A 2026 call-center outsourcing guide from BPO Contact describes outsourcing as a model in which the provider recruits, trains, manages and quality-controls the operation while the client defines the required outcomes. [BPO Contact](https://bpo-contact.com/hi/blog/how-to-outsource-call-center)

## Why companies outsource call center operations

Common reasons include:

- Lower operating costs
- Faster access to agents
- 24/7 coverage
- Seasonal flexibility
- Multilingual support
- Better workforce management
- Access to contact-center technology
- Reduced recruitment burden
- Faster geographic expansion

Outsourcing can be especially useful when customer demand changes faster than your internal hiring process.

## Step 1: Define the call-center workload

Before approaching providers, document your workload.

Measure:

- Calls per day
- Calls per hour
- Peak-hour volume
- Average handle time
- Abandonment
- Service level
- Transfer rate
- First-call resolution
- Escalation rate
- Call reasons
- Operating hours

### Separate inbound and outbound work

Inbound work might include:

- Customer service
- Technical support
- Billing
- Orders
- Complaints

Outbound work might include:

- Sales
- Renewals
- Appointment confirmation
- Customer surveys
- Collections
- Follow-ups

The staffing and pricing model can be very different for each.

## Step 2: Decide what should remain internal

You do not have to outsource the entire call center.

Keep strategic work internal if it requires:

- Deep product knowledge
- Executive escalation
- Sensitive customer relationships
- Product feedback
- Complex technical diagnosis

Outsource routine work if it is:

- Repetitive
- Documented
- High-volume
- Predictable
- Easy to quality-check

## Step 3: Choose your delivery model

### Dedicated agents

A team works primarily for your program.

**Good for:** specialized products, higher volumes and strong brand consistency.

### Shared agents

Agents support multiple clients.

**Good for:** smaller or predictable workloads.

### Overflow

The provider takes calls only when your internal capacity is full.

**Good for:** peaks and seasonal demand.

### 24/7 outsourced coverage

The provider handles nights, weekends and holidays.

**Good for:** global customers and urgent support.

### Hybrid

Internal and outsourced teams share responsibility.

**Good for:** organizations that want flexibility without giving up control of complex cases.

## Step 4: Decide where the operation should be located

Possible delivery models include:

- Onshore
- Nearshore
- Offshore
- Multi-country

Africa can be attractive for cost-sensitive programs. A 2026 BPO cost index gives fully loaded voice benchmarks of approximately **$7–12/hour for Kenya, $9–15 for Egypt, $9–17 for South Africa, $10–15 for Tunisia and $14–18 for Morocco**. [RethinkCX BPO Cost Index](https://www.rethinkcx.com/resources/bpo-cost-index)

These are planning benchmarks, not universal quotes.

Location selection should also consider:

- Language
- Accent
- Time zone
- Talent availability
- Connectivity
- Power reliability
- Security
- Political/business continuity
- Data regulations

## Step 5: Create your vendor scorecard

Score providers on:

| Area | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Cost | 15% |
| Relevant experience | 15% |
| Quality management | 15% |
| Staffing capability | 10% |
| Technology | 10% |
| Security | 10% |
| Scalability | 10% |
| Business continuity | 5% |
| References | 5% |
| Cultural/brand fit | 5% |

Change the weights to fit your operation.

## Step 6: Ask providers for evidence

Do not accept "we can do that" as proof.

Request:

- Relevant case studies
- Client references
- Sample QA reports
- Attrition data
- Training curriculum
- Supervisor ratios
- Sample dashboards
- Business-continuity plan
- Security documentation
- Technology architecture

The strongest providers can demonstrate how they run similar programs.

## Step 7: Agree on service levels

Your SLA should cover measurable targets such as:

- Answer speed
- Service level
- Abandonment
- First-call resolution
- QA score
- CSAT
- Schedule adherence
- Escalation response
- Reporting frequency

Also define what happens if targets are repeatedly missed.

## Step 8: Confirm the technology stack

A modern call center may require:

- Cloud telephony
- IVR
- Call recording
- CRM integration
- Ticketing
- Workforce management
- Quality management
- Analytics
- Knowledge base
- AI-assisted agent tools

Ask who owns the data and what happens to it at contract termination.

## Step 9: Prepare the transition

Create a transition checklist.

### People

- Hiring
- Background checks
- Training
- Certification
- Scheduling

### Process

- Call flows
- Escalations
- Scripts
- Policies
- Exception handling

### Technology

- Accounts
- Integrations
- Phone numbers
- Routing
- Recording
- Reporting

### Governance

- Daily launch meetings
- Weekly reviews
- QA calibration
- Incident process

## Step 10: Run a controlled pilot

Start small.

For example:

- 5–10 agents
- One queue
- One country
- One language
- Limited hours

Monitor actual results against the internal baseline.

## Step 11: Scale gradually

Once the pilot meets its targets, increase:

- Volume
- Seats
- Hours
- Languages
- Queues

Avoid expanding faster than the vendor's training and management system can support.

## How to maintain quality

A call center should be managed through a balanced scorecard.

Track:

**Customer:** CSAT, complaints, resolution

**Quality:** QA score, compliance, call accuracy

**Productivity:** handle time, occupancy, contacts/hour

**Workforce:** attendance, adherence, attrition

**Commercial:** cost per contact, cost per resolution

Avoid optimizing one metric at the expense of all others.

## FAQ

### When should a company outsource its call center?

Usually when volume, coverage or staffing complexity becomes difficult to manage internally. Outsourcing can also make sense for rapid growth, seasonal peaks and multilingual support.

### Is offshore call-center outsourcing safe?

It can be, provided the provider has appropriate security controls, access management, monitoring, contractual protections and business-continuity processes.

### Should I outsource inbound or outbound calls first?

Inbound is often easier to standardize, but outbound programs can also be strong candidates when scripts, compliance and campaign goals are clearly defined.

### How many agents do I need to outsource?

That depends on volume, average handle time, service level, operating hours and shrinkage. A workforce-management model should determine the required staffing rather than using a simple calls-to-agents ratio.

## Final takeaway

To **outsource call center operations** successfully, treat the project as an operating-model transformation.

Define the work, establish your baseline, choose the right delivery model, vet providers, normalize pricing, lock down security, define measurable SLAs, run a pilot and scale carefully.

The best outsourcing relationship is not the one with the lowest quote. It is the one that consistently delivers the required customer outcome at a predictable total cost.
