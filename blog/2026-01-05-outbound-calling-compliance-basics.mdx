Outbound calling is one of the most effective ways to start sales conversations. It is also one of the most regulated. The rules differ by country, by whether you are calling consumers or businesses, and by how the call is made. Getting them wrong can mean fines, complaints and a damaged brand, and in some markets the fines are per call.

This is not legal advice, and you should confirm the rules for your specific campaign with a lawyer who knows your markets. But every business that outsources calling should understand the basics well enough to ask its provider the right questions.

## United States

US telemarketing is governed mainly by two sets of federal rules: the Telephone Consumer Protection Act (TCPA), enforced by the FCC, and the Telemarketing Sales Rule (TSR), enforced by the FTC. Several states add stricter rules of their own.

The points every calling program must handle:

- **The National Do Not Call Registry.** Telemarketing calls to consumers must not go to numbers on the registry unless an exception applies, such as an existing business relationship or the consumer's written permission. Calling lists must be scrubbed against the registry regularly; the TSR requires it at least every 31 days.
- **Your own do-not-call list.** If someone asks not to be called again, that request must be recorded and honored promptly, regardless of the national registry.
- **Calling hours.** Telemarketing calls are not permitted before 8am or after 9pm in the recipient's local time. With national lists, that means checking the time zone of every number, not just your own.
- **Autodialers and prerecorded messages.** Calls to mobile phones using autodialing technology or prerecorded or artificial voices generally require the recipient's prior consent, and prior express written consent for marketing. This is where the largest TCPA claims come from.
- **Caller ID.** Telemarketers must transmit accurate caller ID information, including a number the recipient can call back.
- **Identification.** Agents must identify the seller and the purpose of the call early.

Business-to-business calls are treated more leniently in some respects, but not all. Calls to a business owner's mobile phone, for example, can still fall under consent rules. Assume the rules apply until a lawyer tells you otherwise.

## United Kingdom

UK marketing calls are governed by the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations (PECR) alongside UK GDPR, and enforced by the Information Commissioner's Office.

- **The Telephone Preference Service (TPS)** lists individuals who do not want marketing calls, and the **Corporate TPS** does the same for businesses. Live marketing calls must be screened against both, unless the person has specifically consented to calls from you.
- **Automated marketing calls** with recorded messages require prior specific consent.
- **Callers must identify themselves** and, if asked, provide a contact address or freephone number.
- **Opt-outs must be honored** and recorded, so the person is not called again.

## European Union

Across the EU, GDPR governs how personal data is collected and used for calling, and national rules derived from the ePrivacy Directive govern the calls themselves. Those national rules vary more than many businesses expect. Some countries allow consumer marketing calls unless the person has opted out; others, such as Germany, require prior consent for calls to consumers.

Under GDPR you also need a lawful basis for holding and using the contact data, a clear privacy notice, and a way to honor requests to access or delete it. Buying a list does not transfer the seller's lawful basis to you.

## Call recording

Most call centers record calls for quality and training. The rules on recording are separate from telemarketing rules:

- **In the US,** some states require only one party's consent to record a call, while others, including California, Florida and Washington, require all parties to consent. When calling across states, the safest practice is to announce the recording at the start of every call.
- **In the UK and EU,** you need a lawful basis for recording and must tell callers it is happening and why.

## What to require from a calling provider

Whoever makes the calls on your behalf, the compliance obligations usually still reach you as the seller. Before any campaign starts, ask your provider to show you:

1. **How lists are scrubbed** against national registries, and how often
2. **How opt-out requests are captured** and passed back to you
3. **How calling hours are enforced** by the recipient's time zone
4. **What dialing technology they use,** and how consent is checked where it is required
5. **The exact opening and recording disclosure** agents will use
6. **How recordings and personal data are stored,** who can access them, and for how long
7. **What training agents receive** on these rules, and how it is checked

A good provider will answer all seven without needing to look anything up. If they treat the questions as unusual, treat that as your answer.
