Choosing an e-commerce platform decides more than how your store looks. It decides what you pay every month, how much control you have over checkout, how easily you can grow, and how much technical maintenance you take on. Moving platforms later is possible, but it is a project in itself, with product data, customer accounts, order history and search rankings to migrate.

Three broad options cover most businesses: Shopify, WooCommerce, and a custom or headless build. Here is how they compare.

## Shopify

Shopify is a hosted platform. You pay a monthly subscription, and Shopify runs the servers, security, checkout and core commerce features.

**Strengths**

- Fast to launch, with polished themes and a reliable, well-tested checkout
- Hosting, security and updates are handled for you
- A large app ecosystem for reviews, subscriptions, marketing, shipping and more
- Scales well with traffic without you managing infrastructure

**Weaknesses**

- Monthly costs grow with your plan and the apps you add, and many apps are themselves subscriptions
- Additional transaction fees apply if you use a payment provider other than Shopify's own, where that is available
- Checkout and some core behaviors can only be customized within Shopify's limits, particularly on lower plans
- You are tied to the platform's rules and pricing

**Choose Shopify if** you want to sell quickly with minimal technical overhead, your requirements are fairly standard, and you prefer a predictable subscription to managing hosting.

## WooCommerce

WooCommerce is a free, open-source e-commerce plugin for WordPress. You host it yourself and extend it with plugins.

**Strengths**

- No platform subscription; you pay for hosting and any paid extensions
- Full control over the code, data and hosting
- Excellent if you already run a content-heavy WordPress site
- Highly customizable, with many developers familiar with it

**Weaknesses**

- You are responsible for hosting, performance, security and updates
- Many useful features need paid extensions, and too many plugins hurt speed and stability
- Performance at high traffic or with large catalogs depends heavily on hosting and setup
- Updates to WordPress, WooCommerce and extensions need testing to avoid conflicts

**Choose WooCommerce if** content is central to how you sell, you want full ownership of your platform, and you have reliable development support for maintenance.

## Custom or headless builds

A custom build means a store developed specifically for your business. Today this is often "headless": a commerce engine handles products, carts, orders and payments behind the scenes, while a separate, custom front end, often built with a framework such as Next.js, handles everything customers see. The commerce engine can be Shopify itself, through its Storefront API, or another commerce platform.

**Strengths**

- Complete freedom over design, customer experience and performance
- Can handle unusual requirements: complex product configuration, B2B pricing, marketplaces, subscriptions with custom rules, deep integrations with internal systems
- Very fast storefronts when built well, which helps both conversion and search
- The commerce back end can be swapped without rebuilding the front end, and vice versa

**Weaknesses**

- Highest initial cost and longest build time
- Needs ongoing development capacity rather than an app store
- More moving parts to monitor and maintain

**Choose a custom or headless build if** your business model does not fit standard platforms, the online store is central to your revenue, or speed and customer experience give you a real competitive advantage.

## Side by side

| | Shopify | WooCommerce | Custom or headless |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Time to launch | Fast | Moderate | Slowest |
| Initial cost | Low to medium | Low to medium | High |
| Monthly costs | Subscription plus apps | Hosting plus extensions | Hosting plus development |
| Technical maintenance | Low | High | Medium to high |
| Customization | Within platform limits | High | Unlimited |
| Data and platform control | Moderate | Full | Full |

## Questions that decide it

1. **How standard is your product and checkout?** The more standard, the stronger the case for Shopify.
2. **How important is content?** Stores that sell through articles, guides and SEO often suit WooCommerce or a headless setup.
3. **Who will maintain the store?** Without regular developer support, avoid options that need it.
4. **What are your three-year costs?** Compare subscriptions, apps, transaction fees, hosting and development time over several years, not just the build quote. Our guide to [website development costs](https://zemenaytech.com/blog/website-development-cost-2026) covers the factors.
5. **Which systems must it connect to?** Inventory, accounting, ERP, CRM and shipping integrations often decide the platform on their own.
