# How Much Does It Cost to Run a Call Center in Ethiopia?

**Call center cost Ethiopia** is one of the more difficult operating costs to estimate because a call center is much more than a collection of agent salaries. Your real budget includes recruitment, training, supervisors, telephony, CRM software, internet connectivity, office space, electricity, quality assurance, workforce management, and the cost of keeping enough staff available when demand changes.

{/* truncate */}

In 2026, Ethiopia is attracting more attention as a low-cost BPO and customer-support destination. Local salary estimates put call-center/customer-service roles around **ETB 7,000–15,000 per month**, while another 2026 employment-cost benchmark estimates an English-speaking customer-support representative at about **ETB 18,000 plus employer contributions**, illustrating how dramatically pay can vary by employer, language, shift pattern, and international-client requirements. [Aksion](https://www.aksion.et/guides/common-job-salaries-in-ethiopia) and [Playroll](https://www.playroll.com/employment-cost/ethiopia) both stress that these are indicative rather than universal rates.

For a buyer or operator, the key lesson is simple: **low labor cost does not automatically equal low total operating cost**. The right model is the one that delivers the required service level at a predictable cost per productive contact.

## What is the typical call center cost in Ethiopia?

There is no single national tariff for running a call center. A useful planning approach is to separate the cost into three layers:

1. **Direct people cost** — agents, supervisors, QA, trainers and operations staff.
2. **Delivery infrastructure** — office, computers, headsets, internet, power backup, telephony and software.
3. **Management and risk** — recruitment, attrition, training time, compliance, reporting and contingency capacity.

For a small in-house operation, an initial planning budget might look like this:

| Cost area | Illustrative planning range | Notes |
|---|---:|---|
| Agent pay | ETB 7,000–18,000+ / month | International English, night shifts and specialist work can be higher |
| Employer contributions | Additional | Depends on employment structure and applicable obligations |
| Supervisor | Higher than agent rate | Often one supervisor per several agents |
| Training | One-time + ongoing | Product, language, systems and QA training |
| CRM/help desk | Per-user or tiered | Depends on platform |
| Telephony | Usage + platform | Numbers, minutes, recording and routing |
| Internet | Monthly | Redundant links are preferable for serious operations |
| Power backup | Capex + maintenance | Particularly important for continuity |
| Office | Monthly | Rent, utilities, furniture and security |
| QA/management | Fixed + variable | Needed for consistent service quality |

These ranges are deliberately planning figures rather than a vendor quote. Ethiopia's employment market varies substantially by city, language, seniority and whether the work serves local or international customers.

## A simple 25-agent Ethiopia call center budget

Suppose you want to operate a 25-agent English-language support center.

A basic model could be:

- 25 agents
- 3–4 team leaders/supervisory roles
- 1 operations manager shared across the program
- QA/training capacity
- CRM/help-desk software
- cloud telephony
- redundant internet
- office and power backup

If the average agent compensation were **ETB 18,000/month**, direct agent payroll would be:

**25 × ETB 18,000 = ETB 450,000/month**

That is not the total call center cost.

Once supervisors, employer contributions, recruitment, training, software, telecom, facilities and management are added, the actual operating budget can be materially higher. For this reason, procurement teams should never compare a vendor's per-agent quote with an Ethiopian employee's salary alone.

## Why Ethiopia can be attractive for call centers

Ethiopia has several characteristics that can support a cost-efficient operation:

### 1. Lower labor costs

Compared with mature outsourcing hubs and Western markets, Ethiopian labor costs can be substantially lower. That creates room to offer competitive pricing while still paying above local averages for strong language and customer-service skills.

### 2. A large young workforce

A large working-age population creates a potentially deep recruitment pool. The challenge is not simply finding people; it is finding enough candidates with the right language, digital, communication and customer-service skills.

### 3. Addis Ababa as an operating hub

Addis Ababa is the country's main business and technology center, making it the natural starting point for many formal customer-support operations.

### 4. BPO development opportunity

Ethiopia remains a younger outsourcing market than South Africa, Egypt or Kenya. That can mean lower labor costs, but it also means buyers need to evaluate infrastructure, management maturity, business continuity and vendor experience carefully.

## The hidden costs that change call center cost Ethiopia

### Recruitment and attrition

Hiring an agent costs more than posting a vacancy. You may pay recruiters, conduct assessments, run background checks, provide training and absorb several weeks of reduced productivity.

If turnover is high, recruitment becomes a recurring operating expense.

### Training

International support requires more than basic call-center training. Agents may need:

- Product knowledge
- CRM training
- Written English
- Accent and communication coaching
- Data-security training
- Escalation procedures
- Quality-score calibration

### Shrinkage

Not every paid hour is a productive customer-contact hour. Breaks, meetings, training, leave, absence, coaching and system downtime all reduce productive capacity.

A center that needs 20 productive agents may therefore need more than 20 people on payroll.

### Technology

A modern operation may need:

- Cloud contact-center software
- CRM
- Ticketing
- Knowledge base
- Call recording
- Workforce management
- Quality-assurance tools
- Analytics
- Headsets and computers
- Secure network access

The cheapest technology stack is not always the cheapest operation. Downtime and manual work can quickly erase labor savings.

### Connectivity and power resilience

For voice support, reliable connectivity is a core production asset. Serious operations should evaluate redundant internet, power backup and disaster-recovery arrangements before signing a client contract.

## In-house versus outsourced call center costs

Running your own center gives you direct control but transfers every operational responsibility to your company.

Outsourcing moves many of those costs into a vendor's rate.

| Cost | In-house | Outsourced |
|---|---|---|
| Agent recruitment | Your responsibility | Usually vendor-managed |
| Payroll | Your responsibility | Included in rate |
| Supervisors | Your responsibility | Usually included |
| Office | Your responsibility | Usually included |
| Technology | Your responsibility | Often included |
| Training | Your responsibility | Usually included, depending on scope |
| Scaling | Slower | Usually faster |
| Direct control | High | Medium |
| Operational risk | Internal | Shared with vendor |

The correct comparison is therefore **fully loaded internal cost versus the vendor's fully loaded delivery price**.

## Is Ethiopia cheaper than other African call-center markets?

Not necessarily in every scenario.

A 2026 BPO cost index places typical voice outsourcing around **$7–12/hour in Kenya**, **$9–15/hour in Egypt**, **$9–17/hour in South Africa**, **$10–15/hour in Tunisia**, and **$14–18/hour in Morocco**. The index describes these as fully loaded vendor rates, including wages, benefits, management, tools and facilities. [RethinkCX](https://www.rethinkcx.com/resources/bpo-cost-index)

Ethiopia can be highly competitive on labor, but a smaller BPO ecosystem means the cheapest headline wage does not automatically translate into the lowest client-facing rate.

## How to calculate your own budget

Use this formula:

**Total monthly call center cost = people + technology + telecom + facilities + management + recruitment/training + contingency**

Then calculate:

**Cost per productive hour = total monthly cost ÷ productive agent hours**

This second metric is more useful than salary alone.

### Example

If a 25-agent operation costs ETB 1,000,000 in total each month and produces 3,200 productive agent-hours:

**ETB 1,000,000 ÷ 3,200 = ETB 312.50 per productive hour**

That is the number you should use when comparing operating models.

## When Ethiopia makes sense

Ethiopia is worth considering when:

- Labor economics are a major priority.
- You can invest in training.
- You need English-language support or selected multilingual services.
- You are comfortable developing a newer BPO ecosystem.
- You have a strong local operations partner.
- You can build resilient connectivity and power arrangements.

It requires more diligence when you need mature enterprise BPO infrastructure, highly specialized multilingual support, strict global certifications or a large immediately scalable operation.

## FAQ

### What is the average call center salary in Ethiopia?

2026 public estimates vary considerably. One local salary guide places call-center/customer-service roles at roughly **ETB 7,000–15,000 per month**, while an international employment-cost guide estimates a customer-support representative around **ETB 18,000 plus employer contributions**. The difference demonstrates why buyer-specific compensation data is more useful than a single national average.

### Is Ethiopia a good BPO destination?

It can be, particularly for cost-sensitive operations willing to invest in training and operational controls. Ethiopia is less mature as an outsourcing destination than several established African hubs, so vendor due diligence matters.

### How much does a 25-seat call center cost?

It depends on whether you mean payroll or fully loaded operating cost. A 25-agent operation can have direct agent payroll in the hundreds of thousands of birr per month, but the final budget must also include supervisors, benefits/contributions, technology, telecom, facilities, training and management.

### What is the cheapest way to run a call center?

The cheapest sustainable model is not necessarily the lowest-salary model. It is the model with the lowest **cost per productive, quality-controlled customer interaction**.

## Final takeaway

The best way to understand **call center cost Ethiopia** is to stop treating agent salary as the whole budget.

Ethiopia can offer attractive labor economics, but successful call centers still require investment in training, management, technology, connectivity, power resilience and quality assurance.

For a buyer comparing Ethiopia with other African destinations, request a **fully loaded price per productive hour**, not just a salary or headline hourly rate. That makes the comparison much more meaningful.
