Most companies decide to redesign their website for the same reason: someone looked at it and felt embarrassed. The design looks dated next to a competitor's, the homepage no longer describes what the business does, or the marketing team has given up asking for changes because each one takes weeks.

That feeling is a reasonable starting point, but it is not a plan. A full redesign is expensive, slow and risky, and sometimes a focused refresh solves the real problem in a fraction of the time. Here is how to tell which one you need.

## Refresh, redesign and rebuild are different projects

**A refresh** keeps the existing structure and technology and updates what visitors see: new photography, updated copy, a cleaner type scale, better calls to action, a few rebuilt sections. It usually takes weeks.

**A redesign** rethinks the structure and design: new navigation, new page templates, new content strategy. It may or may not change the underlying platform.

**A rebuild** replaces the technology underneath, either because the current platform cannot do what you need or because it has become too slow, fragile or expensive to maintain. Rebuilds often come with a redesign, but they do not have to.

Separating these three ideas is the most useful thing you can do before talking to anyone about budget.

## Signs a refresh is enough

- The site works well technically: it is fast, secure and easy to update
- Your offer and audience have not changed much
- Visitors find what they need, but the design feels dated
- Specific pages underperform, rather than the whole site
- Your brand has been updated and the site needs to match

In these cases, put the money into content, design polish and the pages that matter most, usually the homepage, key service pages and the contact or pricing page.

## Signs you need a redesign

- Your business has changed: new services, new markets, a different customer
- Visitors cannot find key information, which shows up in analytics as high exits on navigation pages and in sales calls as repeated basic questions
- The site does not work properly on phones
- Conversion rates are poor across the whole site, not just a page or two
- Your team works around the site rather than with it

## Signs you need a rebuild

- Every change needs a developer, even simple text edits
- The site is slow, and attempts to speed it up have stalled
- Plugins, themes or frameworks are out of date and cannot easily be updated
- You need functionality the current platform cannot provide, such as integrations, member areas or custom tools
- Hosting or platform costs keep rising without improvement

If you are rebuilding, the platform choice matters as much as the design. Decide who will edit the site, what it must integrate with, and who will maintain it, before choosing the technology.

## Start with evidence, not opinions

Before deciding, spend a week collecting facts:

- **Analytics:** which pages get traffic, where visitors leave, which pages lead to enquiries or sales
- **Search data:** which pages and queries already bring visitors from Google, so you protect them
- **Customer and sales feedback:** what questions people ask that the site should have answered
- **Content inventory:** every page, whether it is still accurate, and whether anyone visits it
- **Technical review:** speed, mobile experience, security updates and how hard it is to edit

This evidence usually shrinks the project. Many sites have dozens of pages nobody visits, and a handful that do almost all the work.

## Protect what already works

The biggest risk in any redesign is losing search traffic. Pages that rank today rank because of their URL, content and links. Change all three at once and rankings can drop for months.

- Keep URLs the same wherever possible
- Where URLs must change, map every old URL to its new equivalent with permanent redirects
- Keep the content that ranks, improving it rather than replacing it
- Launch, then watch Search Console closely for the first weeks
- Resubmit your XML sitemap and check that no page is accidentally marked noindex

## Plan it in phases

A redesign does not have to launch all at once. A phased plan reduces risk and shows results sooner:

1. **Foundations:** design system, navigation, homepage and the top service pages
2. **Conversion pages:** pricing, contact, booking and landing pages
3. **Content:** blog, resources and case studies, migrated and improved
4. **Long tail:** everything else, or a decision to retire it

Each phase can go live on its own, with analytics showing whether it worked before you move on.
