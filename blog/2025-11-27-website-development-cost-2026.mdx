Ask five agencies to quote the same website and you will often get five prices that differ by a factor of ten. That is not because four of them are dishonest. It is because "a website" can mean anything from a three-page brochure on a template to a custom platform with user accounts, payments and integrations, and most briefs do not say which.

This guide explains what actually drives the cost of a business website in 2026, gives rough ranges for common types of site, and covers the ongoing costs that rarely appear in the first quote.

## What drives the price

A website's cost is mostly the cost of people's time. These factors decide how much time is needed.

**Number of unique page templates.** Not the number of pages, but the number of distinct layouts. A site with fifty blog posts and five templates is cheaper to build than a site with fifteen pages that each look different.

**Custom design or template.** A purchased theme adapted to your brand is far quicker than a design created from scratch, but it looks like other sites using the same theme and is harder to change later.

**Content management.** If your team needs to edit pages, publish posts or manage products without a developer, the site needs a content management system (CMS) configured for exactly that.

**Integrations.** Each connection to another system, such as a CRM, booking tool, payment provider, marketing platform or internal database, adds design, development and testing time.

**Functionality.** User accounts, search, filters, calculators, multi-step forms, member areas and dashboards move a site from "website" toward "web application", with a cost to match.

**E-commerce.** Product catalogs, checkout, payments, shipping rules, tax and inventory add significant scope, even on platforms built for it.

**Languages and accessibility.** Each additional language multiplies content and testing work. Meeting accessibility standards properly takes deliberate design and testing time.

**Content.** Copywriting, photography, video and illustrations are often forgotten in the budget and often the reason launches slip.

## Rough ranges by type of site

The figures below are broad ranges commonly seen in quotes for professional work, not fixed prices. Where the team is based has a large effect: the same scope typically costs considerably less from an experienced offshore team than from an agency in a major Western city.

| Type of site | What it usually includes | Typical range (USD) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Template site | 5 to 10 pages on a theme, basic CMS, contact form | $1,000 to $5,000 |
| Custom marketing site | Custom design, 5 to 20 templates, CMS, SEO setup, integrations | $5,000 to $30,000 |
| E-commerce store | Catalog, checkout, payments, shipping, product management | $5,000 to $50,000+ |
| Web application | Accounts, dashboards, custom logic, APIs | $25,000 to six figures |

A do-it-yourself site builder can cost only a monthly subscription, but you pay in your own time and in limits on what the site can do later.

## The costs after launch

The build is a one-off cost. These are not:

- **Domain name:** usually a small annual fee
- **Hosting:** from free tiers for simple static sites to a meaningful monthly bill for busy or complex ones
- **Platform and plugin subscriptions:** site builders, premium themes, e-commerce platforms, form tools, search
- **Maintenance:** security updates, dependency upgrades, backups and fixes, often on a monthly retainer
- **Content and marketing:** a website that never changes slowly loses search visibility and relevance

A useful rule is to budget each year for ongoing costs that are a meaningful fraction of the original build, rather than assuming the site is finished on launch day.

## Hidden costs that blow up budgets

- **Unclear scope.** The most expensive words in web development are "while you're at it". Changes after design approval cost far more than changes before it.
- **Late content.** Developers waiting for copy and images are still being paid, or the launch date moves.
- **Migration.** Moving content, products, users or old URLs from an existing site is real work, and getting redirects wrong can cost you search traffic.
- **Too many reviewers.** Every additional decision-maker adds review rounds.

## How to lower the cost without lowering quality

1. **Reduce the number of unique templates.** Reusable sections and components cut design and build time significantly.
2. **Launch in phases.** Ship the pages that matter most first, then add the rest.
3. **Prepare content early.** Have copy and images ready before development starts.
4. **Write a clear brief.** Precise requirements get precise quotes and fewer surprises.
5. **Consider an experienced offshore team.** Lower labor costs mean the same budget buys more care, more testing and better design, not just a smaller invoice.
