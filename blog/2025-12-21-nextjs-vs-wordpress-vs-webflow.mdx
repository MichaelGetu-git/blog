Choosing a platform is one of the first decisions in any website project, and one of the hardest to reverse. Three options come up in most conversations today: WordPress, the long-standing content management system behind a large share of the web; Webflow, the visual builder popular with designers; and Next.js, the React framework used for fast, custom-built sites.

Each is an excellent choice for some businesses and a poor one for others. Here is how they compare, and how to decide.

## The short version

- **WordPress** suits content-heavy sites with non-technical editors and a modest budget, where plugins cover most of what you need.
- **Webflow** suits marketing sites where designers want full visual control and nobody wants to manage servers or updates.
- **Next.js** suits businesses that need speed, custom functionality, integrations or a site that will grow into a web application.

## WordPress

WordPress is open-source software you install on your own hosting, with thousands of themes and plugins for almost any feature.

**Strengths**

- Familiar, easy editing for content teams
- A huge ecosystem of plugins for forms, SEO, e-commerce, memberships and more
- Many developers know it, so it is easy to find help
- Low starting cost for simple sites

**Weaknesses**

- Plugins add complexity, slow down pages and each needs updating
- Popularity makes it a common target for attacks, so updates and security hardening are mandatory
- Performance depends heavily on hosting, theme and plugin choices
- Heavily customized themes can become hard to maintain

**Choose WordPress if** you publish a lot of content, your editors are non-technical, your needs are mostly covered by well-maintained plugins, and you will pay for ongoing maintenance.

## Webflow

Webflow is a hosted visual builder. Designers create layouts in a visual editor, and Webflow generates the code and hosts the site.

**Strengths**

- Designers can build precise, polished layouts without writing code
- Hosting, security updates and SSL are handled for you
- A built-in CMS for blogs, case studies and other repeated content
- Fast to launch and easy for marketers to make page changes

**Weaknesses**

- Subscription costs rise with traffic, CMS size and features
- Complex functionality and custom logic are limited or need workarounds
- You are tied to the platform; moving away means rebuilding
- Advanced integrations often depend on third-party tools

**Choose Webflow if** your site is primarily a marketing site, design quality matters a lot, your team wants to edit without developers, and you do not need complex custom features.

## Next.js

Next.js is a framework for building websites and applications with React. Developers write the site in code, often connected to a headless CMS such as Sanity, Contentful, Strapi or Payload for content editing.

**Strengths**

- Excellent performance when built well, which helps search rankings and conversions
- No limits on functionality, design or integrations
- Pages can be pre-rendered for speed and search engines, with dynamic features where needed
- The same codebase can grow from a marketing site into a full web application
- A smaller attack surface than a plugin-heavy CMS

**Weaknesses**

- Requires developers to build and to change anything structural
- Content editing depends on how the CMS is set up; a poor setup frustrates editors
- Higher initial cost than a template on WordPress or Webflow

**Choose Next.js if** you need custom features or integrations, performance is a priority, you plan to add application features over time, or your site is central to how you sell.

For full disclosure: our own website runs on Next.js, because we wanted complete control over performance, structure and integrations. That does not make it the right choice for everyone.

## Side by side

| | WordPress | Webflow | Next.js |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Initial build cost | Low to medium | Low to medium | Medium to high |
| Ongoing cost | Hosting plus maintenance | Subscription | Hosting plus development |
| Editing for non-technical staff | Easy | Easy | Depends on CMS setup |
| Custom functionality | Through plugins | Limited | Unlimited |
| Performance | Varies widely | Good | Excellent when built well |
| Maintenance burden | High | Low | Medium |
| Platform lock-in | Low | High | Low |

## SEO is not a platform feature

All three can rank well. Search performance depends far more on content, site structure, speed and technical setup than on the platform itself. A fast, well-structured WordPress site beats a slow, badly built Next.js one. Whatever you choose, the foundations are the same: fast pages on mobile, clean URLs, redirects from any old pages, an XML sitemap, sensible titles and headings, and structured data. The quality of the build matters more than the logo on the platform.

## How to decide

Answer these honestly:

1. Who will update the site, and how often?
2. What does the site need to do beyond showing content, now and in two years?
3. Who will maintain it after launch?
4. What is the real budget for the build and for the next three years?
