# Kubernetes Is Overkill for 95% of Startups: Developers Can Escape the Cloud Complexity Tax

If there are five thousand users that are actively using the application every day, it is like buying a commercial freight train to take a single pizza.

A common expensive architectural slip that one can make in early and growth stage Distributed Engineering Teams is orchestrating containers. Organizations that are experiencing a growth phase in their Offshore Software Development experience a push toward emulating the plans of large companies from the outset. Your product teams find thousands of lines of YAML code after shipping product features, bugs with ingress controller, and latency of service mesh/Helm chart updates.

Remote Team Management becomes fire putting while the skill of infra turns from rule of thumb to babysitting the control plane, and the Remote Work Culture shifts from customer iteration to babysitting the control plane.

## The actual price of premature Kube

A production Kubernetes is a costly effort to implement when not using a platform engineering team:

Cluster Upgrade Networking / Persistent Volume Break: Cluster upgrades without ordering of networking or persistent volume upgrades mean that engineers can no longer rely on Asynchronous Work Strategies. They have no choice but to enter the multi time zone war rooms, just to keep up with the basic staging/pod production.

Slowed Onboarding: Each custom operator, cluster dependency prolongs Remote Developer Onboarding. Before making commits to the production code, new hires spend their first 2 weeks trying to get the local versions of minikube and kubectl to work, and get a VPN tunnel working.

Trying to meddle in the dev's time, indirectly, by asking them to act as an SRE, is a form of Remote Talent Retention attack! Top builders don't want to spend Friday night troubleshooting out-of-date certificates.

For instance, security blind spots as a result of misconfigured role-based access controls and too much pod networking can be easily avoided when International Tech Compliance audits are in motion.

## The Pragmatic Scaling Ladder

High performing Distributed Agile Teams scale their infrastructure per their real traffic and not their predicted growth:

At stage 1 - 0 to 50,000 Users - just use the managed Platform-as-a-Service (PaaS) providers or simple managed containers such as AWS ECS Fargate, Render or Fly.io to achieve zero-downtime deployments and auto-scaling, without having to manage any control plane.

Stage 2 (50K to 5M Users): Add Managed databases, Background worker queues, CDN (Cache) and more and more small features. With a standardised deployment pipeline, code can be moved to production with ease in Cross-Cultural Team Integration – everyone in the development team knows how to move it there.

When you're supporting two full-time DevOps engineers to maintain cluster, have strict multi-region residency requirements, and have dozens of independent services, you should consider using Kubernetes only at stage 3 (500,000+ Users & Multi-Service Scale).
