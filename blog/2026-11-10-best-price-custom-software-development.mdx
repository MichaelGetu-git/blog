Every founder who has asked for a software quote knows the feeling. You send the same brief to three companies and three numbers come back: $40,000, $180,000 and $450,000. The gap is so wide that it is hard to believe they read the same document.

So where do you actually go to get the best price for custom software?

The honest answer has two parts. First, "best price" does not mean "lowest quote". It means the lowest total cost of software that ships, works and keeps working. Second, geography is still the biggest pricing lever that most buyers never pull. The same engineering hour can cost three times more in one city than in another, and the gap has very little to do with how good the engineer is.

In this guide we walk through the numbers: the global picture first, then Africa, then Ethiopia, and finally ZemenayTech, the company writing this. We are based in Addis Ababa and we obviously have an interest in the answer, so every statistic below links to its source, and we point out where the data is thin or the headlines overreach.

> **The short version**
>
> - Large IT projects run 45% over budget on average and deliver 56% less value than promised, so the cheapest quote is not automatically the cheapest project.
> - Outsourced development rates run roughly $80-$180+ an hour in North America and Western Europe, versus roughly $25-$65 in Africa.
> - Africa's developer community grew 21% a year between 2019 and 2024, faster than any other continent.
> - Ethiopia is among the fastest-growing developer markets on the continent, with a very young population, a national coding push and a low cost base.
> - The best price comes from a fair hourly rate, a clear scope and low rework. All three, not just the first.

## What "best price" actually means

Most quote comparisons stop at the hourly rate or the project total. That is a bit like choosing a car by the sticker price and ignoring fuel, servicing and breakdowns. A more useful way to think about it:

**Total price = build + rework + running costs + the cost of delay**

Each part of that equation can quietly double your bill.

### Rework and overruns

The most-cited study on the subject comes from [McKinsey and the University of Oxford](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/tech-and-ai/our-insights/delivering-large-scale-it-projects-on-time-on-budget-and-on-value). After analysing more than 5,400 IT projects, the researchers found that large projects run 45% over budget and 7% over schedule on average, while delivering 56% less value than predicted. About 17% went so badly that they threatened the survival of the company that funded them. They also found that every extra year a project is scheduled to run adds roughly 15% to cost overruns.

A fair caveat: that study looked at projects with budgets above $15 million. Your $60,000 customer portal is a different animal. But the direction of the finding holds at every size. When scope is fuzzy and the team is wrong for the job, the invoice grows.

One European cost analysis goes further and claims that very cheap offshore engagements can see 30% to 50% rework costs that wipe out the initial savings. Treat that as one vendor's claim rather than a law of nature, but it describes the real risk well. A low rate attached to a weak team is not a bargain.

### Running costs

Software is not a purchase, it is a pet. Industry guides consistently suggest budgeting [15% to 20% of the original build cost every year](https://www.codestringers.com/articles/software-maintenance-costs-explained) for fixes, security patches, compatibility updates and small improvements. On a $100,000 build, that is $15,000 to $20,000 a year, indefinitely. One 2026 analysis estimates that the [five-year total cost of custom software is typically two to three times the initial build](https://www.easy.bi/blog/custom-software-development-cost-2026/).

### So what is the best price?

It is the lowest total across all four parts of the equation. That gives you three levers to pull:

1. **The rate**: what each hour costs.
2. **The hours**: how clearly the scope is defined and how efficiently the team works.
3. **The rework**: how often the team gets it right the first time.

Geography mostly moves the first lever. Team quality and process move the other two. The smartest buyers pick a region that gives them a lower rate, then spend the savings on better scoping and better people.

At the midpoint, the African price is roughly a third of the North American and Western European one, a saving of about $326,000 on the same number of hours. Against Eastern Europe the saving is closer to 22%.

Two honest notes. First, hours are not identical everywhere: a very senior engineer in an expensive market may finish a task in fewer hours, which narrows the gap a little. Second, the cheapest region on paper is not automatically the best fit. This is exactly why the next question matters: if rate is only one lever, why look at Africa at all?

## Why Africa belongs on your shortlist

### The talent pool is growing faster than anywhere else

According to a March 2026 analysis by [BCG](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/develop-the-developers-a-strategic-priority-for-africa), Africa had about 4.7 million developers in 2024. That is the smallest base of any region except Oceania, but it grew at 21% a year between 2019 and 2024, the fastest of any continent. North America grew at 9.2% over the same period.

![Bar chart of annual developer growth 2019 to 2024: Africa 21 percent, South America 19.8, Asia 16.6, Europe 11.3, Oceania 9.5, North America 9.2](/img/BestPriceCustomSoftwareDevelopment/01-developer-growth-by-continent.svg)

*Average annual growth in developers, 2019-2024. Source: BCG analysis of GitHub data.*

Other trackers show the same direction. GitHub's 2025 Octoverse report found that the Africa and Middle East region [added 3.4 million new developers in a single year](https://openuk.uk/report/africa26/), and OpenUK counted around 9.3 million developer accounts across Africa in the first quarter of 2026. The numbers differ because the definitions differ: BCG and GitHub count students, hobbyists and self-taught coders alongside professionals. But nobody measuring this is finding a shrinking pool.

At the country level, Nigeria's developer community grew [45% in 2023](https://www.andela.com/newsroom/africas-emergence-as-the-worlds-next-it-services-and-ai-talent-destination), the largest jump of any country worldwide that year according to GitHub data. BCG also points out that Ethiopia and Angola grew their developer communities at the fastest rates in the 2019-2024 window, from very small bases.

### The rate advantage is real

As the table above shows, African rates sit in the lowest tier globally, alongside South and Southeast Asia. For a buyer, that is the room in the budget to spend on better scoping, better testing and a longer runway.

### The time zone works, especially for Europe

East Africa runs on UTC+3 all year round. That puts Ethiopia roughly one to three hours ahead of most of Europe depending on the season, which means most of the working day is shared with London, Amsterdam or Berlin. For the US East Coast, Ethiopia is seven to eight hours ahead, which still gives you a few live hours in your morning and a team that has already worked half a day by the time you open your laptop.

### Where Africa is not the whole answer

QArea notes that Africa's senior talent pool is smaller than Eastern Europe's or Asia's, so [access to specialised developers depends heavily on the partner you choose](https://qarea.com/blog/worldwide-software-development-outsourcing-rates). The same guide advises extra scrutiny whenever a quote sits far below the regional average. We agree with both points. A deep pool of junior and mid-level talent is only valuable if someone is vetting, mentoring and managing it. That is the job of a good partner, and it is the reason this guide ends with a checklist for evaluating any vendor, including us.

## Why Ethiopia stands out within Africa

![Six stat cards about Ethiopia: 134 million people with a median age of 19.1, 85.4 million mobile connections, over 94 thousand GitHub developers up 32 percent, a 658 million dollar IT outsourcing market in 2025, a forecast of 1.09 billion dollars by 2029, and over 5 million enrolled in national coder training](/img/BestPriceCustomSoftwareDevelopment/03-ethiopia-at-a-glance.svg)

*Ethiopia at a glance. Sources: DataReportal (via VillPress), GitHub Octoverse 2023, Statista, Government of Ethiopia and UAE.*

### A young country that is learning to code

Ethiopia had roughly 134 million people in early 2025 with a median age of just 19.1, and about 85.4 million mobile connections, according to [DataReportal figures compiled by VillPress](https://villpress.com/?p=5725). Demographics like these mean a talent pipeline that keeps refilling.

The government is leaning into it. The "5 Million Ethiopian Coders" initiative launched in July 2024 in partnership with the UAE. In June 2026 the prime minister [celebrated reaching the five million milestone](https://www.ena.et/web/eng/w/eng_9012882), and the target has since been [expanded to seven million](https://www.wam.ae/en/article/c132rdh-uae-ethiopia-expand-million-ethiopian-coders).

Here is the caveat we promised. Those numbers count enrolments in foundational digital skills training, not working software engineers. Early in the programme, for instance, [about 31,000 people had been certified out of roughly 246,000 enrolled](https://wearetech.africa/en/fils-uk/news/public-management/ethiopia-trains-31-000-young-coders-in-three-months-aiming-for-5-million-by-2026). Local reporting has also noted that certificates and real skills are not the same thing, with [civil servants rushing to collect certificates](https://addisfortune.news/civil-servants-rush-to-secure-digital-certificates-skills-take-a-back-seat). The initiative is a genuine long-term investment in the base of the pyramid. It does not mean 5 million people are ready to build your payment platform.

### The professional talent pool is smaller than the headlines

GitHub's Octoverse 2023 reported [more than 94,000 developers in Ethiopia, growing 32% in a year](https://github.blog/2023-11-08-the-state-of-open-source-and-ai). That is fast growth, but GitHub accounts include students and hobbyists. An older Google-based estimate from 2021 counted around [19,000 professional developers](https://www.scrums.com/blog/african-tech-talent-global-demand-and-opportunities) in the country. The truth sits somewhere between those two figures and moves up every year.

The practical takeaway: there is a lot of promising talent, and finding the strong people takes local knowledge. That gap is where an on-the-ground partner earns its fee.

### The market is scaling

[Statista](https://statista.com/outlook/tmo/it-services/it-outsourcing/ethiopia) projects Ethiopia's IT outsourcing market at about $658 million in 2025, growing around 13.5% a year to roughly $1.09 billion by 2029. Growth like that points to rising demand for software work inside the country as well, which gives local engineers more chances to learn on real projects.

### What a developer costs here

[Payscale](https://www.payscale.com/research/ET/Job=Software_Engineer/Salary) puts the average base salary of an Ethiopian software engineer at about 40,000 birr a month, with the middle half of the market between roughly 22,000 and 53,000 birr. With the birr trading at [about 161 per US dollar in early November 2026](https://www.riotimesonline.com/ethiopia-cost-of-living-expats-2026/), that is about $250 a month on average, or roughly $137 to $329 across the middle of the market.

Please do not read that as a price list. Salary is not a vendor rate. A real vendor price also covers recruitment, management, QA, equipment, backup power and internet, and a margin. Published salary figures also vary widely between sources, and engineers at multinationals earn well above the average. What the data does tell you is that the underlying cost base is low, which is where the room in the hourly rate comes from.

### Currency and inflation: what buyers should know

Ethiopia floated the birr in July 2024. Annual inflation was [14.9% in September 2026 and the central bank held its policy rate at 16%](https://www.riotimesonline.com/ethiopia-central-bank-holds-rate-16-percent-2026/) on 3 November. For a buyer paying in US dollars, this has two implications. The good news is that a dollar contract is shielded from birr swings. The less obvious news is that local wages rise with local inflation, so a responsible vendor will want to review rates once a year. Ask about this up front and write it into the contract. A vendor who says rates will never change is either planning to absorb the cost, or planning to cut corners.

### Language, culture and working hours

English is widely used as the language of instruction in Ethiopian higher education, so many graduates read documentation, write tickets and join stand-ups in English without friction. Combine that with the time zone overlap covered earlier and Ethiopia works well as an extension of a European, Middle Eastern or even North American team.

## How to get a quote you can trust

Whoever you choose, these eight steps will protect your budget:

1. **Write a one-page brief.** Describe the users, the outcome you want, and your constraints. A fuzzy brief is the number one cause of wildly different quotes.
2. **Ask for the price in three parts.** Discovery, build and run. If a vendor will not quote the run, assume it will cost you later.
3. **Ask exactly who will work on it.** Names, seniority and relevant experience. Be wary of "a team of experts" with no faces.
4. **Start with a small paid pilot.** Two to four weeks on a real slice of the product tells you more than any proposal.
5. **Own your code from day one.** Insist on repository access, documentation and intellectual property assigned to you.
6. **Agree on currency and review dates.** Pay in a stable currency and agree when rates will be reviewed, especially in markets with higher inflation.
7. **Collect at least three quotes from at least two regions.** Comparing across geographies shows you what the market really charges.
8. **Budget for the long term.** Set aside 15% to 20% of the build cost every year for maintenance, and treat anything lower as an assumption you are taking on.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does custom software development cost in 2026?

It depends on scope. One 2026 guide puts [simple apps at $15,000 to $50,000, medium projects at $50,000 to $200,000, and complex systems at $200,000 to over $1 million](https://www.vasundhara.io/blogs/custom-software-development-cost-in-2026-after-ai). Where your team is located can move these numbers up or down by a factor of two or three.

### Is outsourcing software development to Africa safe?

It is as safe as the vendor you pick. Check references, meet the developers, run a small paid pilot first and make sure you own your code. The same due diligence applies in Poland, India or Brazil.

### Why Ethiopia and not Kenya, Nigeria or Egypt?

Those are larger and more established hubs, and for some projects they may be the right choice. Ethiopia's case is a low cost base, an extremely young population, fast developer growth and strong national investment in digital skills. The trade-offs are a smaller senior talent pool and currency and inflation dynamics that you should plan for. Ask any Ethiopian vendor about backup power and internet, too.

### Should I choose fixed price or hourly?

Fixed price works when the scope is tightly defined and unlikely to change. Hourly or dedicated-team models work better when you are still learning what the product should be. Many buyers start with a fixed-price discovery phase, then move to a dedicated team for the build.

### What currency would I pay in?

US dollars are the norm for international contracts. Agree on payment terms and an annual rate review in writing.
